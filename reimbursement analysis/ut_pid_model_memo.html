We have prepared the attached reimbursement analysis for Viridian Farm Public Infrastructure District No. 1 (the “District”) as requested by the developer. The analysis sizes a senior / subordinate Utah public infrastructure district financing and a subsequent senior refunding, held within the levy caps that bind a public infrastructure district under the Public Infrastructure District Act, Title 17D, Chapter 4, Utah Code. The following is a summary of the assumptions used in the analysis:

  • Development delivers 716 homes on 716 lots (weighted-average sales price ≈ $449,000); value phases onto the tax roll as homes close.
  • Lot values are estimated at 10% of the average selling price (platted-lot value); a delivered lot’s value rolls off lot-inventory value and the finished home rolls on as each home closes.
  • Utah taxable ratios: 55.00% on primary residential value (the 45% exemption, § 59-2-103) and 55.0% on builder lot inventory, which carries the same exemption where the assessor determines the property will be a primary residence once occupied (Utah Admin. Code R884-24P-52).
  • A district levy of 3.000 mills is assumed — 3.000 mills for debt service. The levy is capped at the most restrictive of 15.000 mills (§ 17D-4-303), the governing document, and the indentures — 3.000 mills here, so 3.000 mills is levied. Utah caps are fixed rates per dollar of taxable value and do not float with the residential exemption, so there is no Colorado-style “Gallagherization” adjustment. Because the required mill levy stays within those caps, the District need not give Truth in Taxation notice or hold a hearing to impose it.
  • County assessors revalue annually (§ 59-2-303.1); value already on the roll grows at 1.0% a year. Value created in a calendar year lands on the following 1 January roll, is billed that November, and pays debt service the next 1 March — a 2-year lag from creation to the payment it supports.
  • Home prices and market values escalate at 3.0% annually.
  • A 98.0% tax-collection rate and a 2.50% interest-earnings rate on fund balances are assumed.
  • Senior new-money bonds carry a 5.88% interest rate, a 29-year final maturity, and are sized to a minimum 1.30x debt-service coverage ratio.
  • The senior debt-service reserve fund is sized to the 3-prong test (least of 10% of par, maximum annual debt service, or 125% of average annual debt service); interest earnings on fund balances offset debt service.
  • Utah property taxes are levied on value as of 1 January and are due in a single payment on 30 November, so district principal is structured on 1 March with interest on 1 March and 1 September — each year’s collections are in hand before the payment they support. Delinquent taxes carry a penalty of the greater of 2.5% or $10, and the Public Infrastructure District Act permits the District to impose an additional 7% annual penalty; no penalty revenue is credited here.
  • No credit is taken for the personal property uniform fee (§ 59-2-405), which would otherwise be distributed to the District in the same proportion as real property tax.
  • County assessing and collecting is funded by a separate statewide levy on property (§ 59-2-1602) rather than a deduction from the District’s distribution, so 0.00% is netted from pledged revenue (a Colorado metropolitan district would lose roughly 1.50% to the county treasurer).
  • Capitalized interest (up to 36 months) is funded from bond proceeds to cover debt service during the initial absorption ramp-up.
  • A subordinate-lien cash-flow note (8.12%, interest accretes) is sized to the residual surplus at a 1.00x coverage factor and repaid from surplus revenues.
  • The senior new-money bonds are refunded at the first optional call (3/1/2029, 103% call price) at 4.50% / 1.20x coverage to generate additional new-money reimbursement.
