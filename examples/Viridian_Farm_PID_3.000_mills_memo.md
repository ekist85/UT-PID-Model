## 2. Security and pledged revenue

The bonds are limited tax general obligations of the District, payable from an ad valorem property tax levied against all taxable property within the District's boundaries. The levy is capped at three levels, the most restrictive of which controls: 15.000 mills (0.015 per dollar of taxable value) under Section 17D-4-303, Utah Code; the rate fixed in the District's governing document; and the rate fixed in the indentures. This analysis assumes the controlling rate is 3.000 mills.

Because the levy securing the required mill levy does not exceed the rate established in the Public Infrastructure District Act, the governing document, or the indentures, the District is not required to give Truth in Taxation notice or hold a public hearing to impose it. That distinguishes a Utah PID levy from a levy above the certified tax rate, and removes the annual political risk a Colorado metropolitan district faces when a service plan cap is approached.

Property taxes in Utah are levied against taxable value determined as of 1 January and are due on 30 November of the same year. Debt service is therefore structured with principal due 1 March and interest semi-annually, so each year's collections are in hand before the payment they support. Delinquent taxes carry a penalty of the greater of 2.5% or $10, and the Act permits the District to impose an additional 7% annual penalty. This analysis assumes collections of 98.00% of the levy and takes no credit for penalty revenue.

- Senior lien coverage requirement: 1.30x of net pledged revenue.
- Subordinate lien coverage requirement: 1.00x; the subordinate bonds are cashflow bonds, paid only from revenue released after senior debt service, with unpaid interest accruing at 8.125%.
- Debt service reserve / surplus fund: $546,023, sized at the least of 10% of par, 125% of average annual debt service, and maximum annual debt service.
- Capitalized interest: $806,662, funding interest through March 1, 2027 (36 months from closing).
- Personal property uniform fees (Section 59-2-405, Utah Code) are distributed to the District in the same proportion as real property tax; this analysis credits 0.00% of the levy.

## 4. Taxable value

Utah taxes primary residential property on 55% of fair market value — the 45% primary residential exemption under Section 59-2-103, Utah Code, which reaches up to one acre of land per residential unit. Finished lots held in builder inventory are carried at the same ratio: Utah Admin. Code R884-24P-52 allows the residential exemption on unoccupied property the county assessor determines will qualify as a primary residence once occupied. This is the single largest difference from the Colorado template, where homes are assessed at the residential rate (6.7% in the reference model) and vacant land at the 29% non-residential rate.

County assessors revalue annually, so value created in one calendar year appears on the following year's roll — a 1-year lag, against the two-year lag Colorado's biennial reassessment cycle produces. Values already on the roll are grown at 1.00% per year. Finished lots are carried at 10.00% of the eventual home price until a home closes on them.

Taxable value first supports a levy in 2024, reaches $172,718,644 at buildout in 2029, and grows to $250,539,604 by 2055 on reassessment alone.

## 5. Sizing results and debt service

Principal is solved backwards from the final maturity: in each year the projected net pledged revenue is divided by the 1.30x coverage requirement, the coupon generated by later maturities is removed, and the balance is turned into principal in $5,000 denominations. The result is $5.69MM of senior bonds with an average life of 22.11 years, final maturity March 1, 2054, maximum annual debt service of $477,902 and total debt service of $11,354,237.

The arbitrage TIC is 5.876% and the all-in TIC, including the underwriters' discount and costs of issuance, is 6.592%. Subordinate bonds of $0.80MM are sized to the largest amount the residual cashflow retires in full — principal and accrued interest — by March 15, 2039. Total debt service across both liens is $15,459,856, a repayment ratio of 3.31x on the reimbursement delivered.

## 7. How this differs from the Colorado model

The model is the Colorado metropolitan district template — same tabs, same layout, same sizing mechanics. The substantive changes are the ones Utah law requires.

**Colorado metropolitan district vs. Utah PID**

| Item | Colorado | Utah (this model) |
|---|---|---|
| Enabling act | Title 32, Article 1, C.R.S. (special districts) | Title 17D, Chapter 4, Utah Code (PID Act) |
| Levy cap | Service plan mill levy cap, adjusted for changes in the residential assessment rate | Least of 15.000 mills by statute, the governing document cap, and the indenture cap — no adjustment mechanism |
| Residential assessment | Gallagher / statutory residential assessment rate (6.7% in the reference model); vacant land at 29% | 45% primary residential exemption → 55.00% of fair market value; builder inventory at the same ratio under R884-24P-52 |
| Levy cap adjustment | Gallagherization of the service plan cap | None — the cap is a fixed rate per dollar of taxable value |
| Reassessment cycle | Biennial | Annual → a 1-year lag from value creation to the tax roll |
| Tax due dates | Half 28 February / half 15 June (or full 30 April) | Single payment, 30 November |
| Principal payment date | 1 December | 1 March |
| Vehicle tax revenue | Specific ownership tax, ~6–8% of the levy | Personal property uniform fee (Section 59-2-405), distributed pro rata; credited at 0.00% |
| County collection cost | County treasurer fee deducted from the distribution (~1.5%) | Recovered through a separate statewide levy on property (Section 59-2-1602); 0.00% deducted here |
| Rate increase procedure | TABOR election for new mill levies | Truth in Taxation hearing above the certified tax rate; the required mill levy is exempt while within the caps |
| Operations levy | Separate operations and maintenance mill levy | None assumed — district administration is charged against pledged revenue |
| Agricultural land | Agricultural classification | Greenbelt Reduction (Section 59-2-503) with up to five years of rollback tax on withdrawal — a Developer obligation |
